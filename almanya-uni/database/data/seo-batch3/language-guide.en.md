> **Short answer:** For a German-taught Bachelor's or Master's, you usually need a German exam certificate at university level. Under the joint framework regulation of the HRK and the KMK, TestDaF with at least TDN 4 in all four parts and DSH-2 count as proof for all programmes; telc Deutsch C1 Hochschule and Goethe-Zertifikat C2 are listed there as exempting certificates. Goethe C1 is not on that list, so whether it counts is up to the university. Which certificate, which level and at which stage is decided by each university and programme.

## Four things that are easy to mix up

When people talk about the German requirement, four different things often get mixed up:

1. **Language level:** CEFR levels such as B1, B2 or C1. A level on its own is not a certificate.
2. **Course attendance certificate:** shows that you attended or completed a course. It is not an exam result, and according to uni-assist many universities do not accept it as proof of German.
3. **Exam certificate:** the result of an exam such as TestDaF, DSH, telc or Goethe.
4. **The certificate the university accepts:** which exam certificate and which result are enough is decided by the university and the programme.

## Which level do you need, and when?

- **Admission to a German-taught programme:** usually a university-level certificate, for example TestDaF with TDN 4 in every part or DSH-2. Lower entry levels (TDN 3, DSH-1) are only enough where the university allows them for that programme.
- **Admission to a German course, not to the degree:** some universities first admit you to a German course or as a language student. That is not the same as admission to the degree programme. We compared the routes using official sources on our page on [universities offering conditional admission](/en/universities/collections/conditional-admission-universities).
- **An English-taught programme:** admission often requires no German, but some programmes expect basic German at a certain stage. TUM's English-taught Aerospace Bachelor's, for example, requires at least A2 at application.

## At application or at enrolment?

When the certificate is due differs between programmes. LMU München makes the difference explicit:

- **In restricted-admission (NC) programmes**, applicants from outside the EU must submit an accepted language certificate by the application deadline; it cannot be handed in later.
- **In admission-free programmes**, a certificate showing that you completed a course can be enough at the application stage, but DSH-2 or another accepted C1/C2 certificate must be in place by enrolment (Immatrikulation). A course registration confirmation is not enough, and provisional results are not accepted.

Other universities can have different rules. On our [conditional admission page](/en/universities/collections/conditional-admission-universities) you can see, for example, the enrolment-stage rules of RWTH Aachen and Universität Hamburg.

## Certificates: what each one is for

| Certificate | What it is used for | Who decides whether it is accepted | What to check on the programme page |
|---|---|---|---|
| TestDaF | Test for university admission, digital or on paper; results as TDN 3, 4 or 5 | In the framework regulation; TDN 4 in all four parts counts as proof for all programmes. The university sets the level for each programme. | The TDN required in each part; whether the university only accepts a recent certificate |
| DSH | University entrance exam offered by universities and recognised Studienkollegs in Germany; DSH-1, DSH-2, DSH-3 | In the framework regulation; a DSH registered with the HRK is recognised by German universities, and DSH-2 counts for all programmes. An admission based on DSH-1 at one university does not bind others. | The DSH level required; whether DSH-1 is accepted |
| telc Deutsch C1 Hochschule | C1 exam designed for university entrance | Listed as an exempting certificate in the framework regulation; individual universities can have different rules | Whether it is on the programme's list |
| Goethe-Zertifikat C2: GDS | The highest Goethe-Institut exam | Listed as an exempting certificate in the framework regulation; recognising older Goethe diplomas is up to the university | The programme's rule if you hold an older Goethe diploma |
| Goethe-Zertifikat C1 | General German at C1 | Not in the framework regulation; the university decides (RWTH Aachen accepts it, for example, while LMU München lists only C2) | Whether Goethe C1 is on the programme's list |
| Course attendance certificate | Shows that you attended or completed a course | Many universities do not accept it as proof; some accept it only at the application stage for admission-free programmes | What is enough at application and what must be handed in by enrolment |

*Framework regulation: the HRK/KMK framework regulation on German language exams for German-taught study at German universities (RO-DT), version of November 2025. The second level of the KMK German Language Diploma (DSD II) is also listed in it.*

### TestDaF or DSH?

Both are in the framework regulation, so the choice often comes down to where and when you can sit the exam. TestDaF is taken at test centres, digitally or on paper. The DSH is run by universities and recognised Studienkollegs in Germany. Check the programme page for the result you need; for TestDaF it may be stated per part.

### telc C1 Hochschule and the Goethe certificates

telc Deutsch C1 Hochschule and Goethe-Zertifikat C2: GDS are listed as exempting certificates in the framework regulation. Goethe-Zertifikat C1 is not; always check whether your target university accepts it.

### Do certificates expire?

According to TestDaF, a TestDaF certificate is valid indefinitely, but some universities only accept a recent one. For other certificates, check the university's rule as well.

## Which certificate should you take?

1. Find out which certificates and levels your programme accepts.
2. Check whether the certificate is due at application or at enrolment.
3. Choose based on where and when you can take the exam. If you are applying from abroad, keep in mind that the DSH is held at universities and Studienkollegs in Germany; an exam taken at a test centre (for example TestDaF or telc) can be more practical before you arrive.
4. A course certificate is not an exam result, so plan which exam you will take when the course ends.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I apply to a German university with B1 German?

For admission to a German-taught degree programme, B1 is usually not enough. Some universities admit you to a German course with B1 or B2, and some admission-free programmes accept the C1 certificate by enrolment; neither is the same as direct admission to the degree.

### What does 4x4 mean for TestDaF?

TDN 4 in each of the four parts (reading, listening, writing, speaking). Under the framework regulation, this result is sufficient proof for admission and enrolment in all programmes, although a university can set a different level for a specific programme.

### If I pass the DSH at one university, will other universities accept it?

A DSH registered with the HRK is recognised by German universities under the framework regulation. An admission based on DSH-1 at one university does not bind other universities, though.

### Can I get into university with Goethe C1?

Goethe C1 is not in the framework regulation, so this is the university's decision. RWTH Aachen accepts it, for example, while LMU München lists only Goethe C2.

### Does a course attendance certificate count as proof of German?

Usually not; according to uni-assist, many universities do not accept it. Some accept a completed-course certificate at the application stage for admission-free programmes but require an exam certificate for enrolment.
